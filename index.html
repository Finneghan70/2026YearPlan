Why "Inquiry into Action"? Inquiry (ask questions, investigate, make sense) and Design Thinking (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test) are not competing frameworks — they are complementary. Inquiry generates the why and what. Design thinking structures the how. Together they avoid inquiry that stays abstract, and design thinking that lacks genuine purpose. BC's curriculum is built for exactly this combination.
Inquiry framework

Students ask authentic questions about the world, investigate through observation and research, make sense of evidence, and communicate their understanding. Driven by curiosity and real questions.

ASKS: What do we wonder? What evidence do we have? What does it mean?
+
Design thinking framework

Students empathize with a real need, define a problem, generate many ideas, build prototypes, and test and improve. Driven by a desire to make something that works for someone.

ASKS: Who needs this? What could we make? Does it work? How can we improve it?
The six phases — Inquiry into Action
One team, one question: Every inquiry unit opens with a single driving question the whole team investigates together. Younger students bring fresh curiosity; older students bring deeper research skills and take on leadership roles in the design phases. Both are essential.
